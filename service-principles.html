— Service Principles

Clear boundaries are part of responsible advisory work.

These principles set out what SBMG does and does not do, how the firm coordinates with qualified professionals, and the standards that govern the firm's engagement with families and institutions.

SBMG provides advisory and coordination services. We do not provide legal, tax, investment or regulated financial advice. Where such expertise is required, we coordinate with qualified professionals.

Where legal immigration matters arise, these are handled exclusively by qualified Luxembourg avocats. SBMG's role in such situations is limited to coordinating the family-side process.

We do not guarantee school admission, residence permit approval, employment outcomes or investment results. Our work is based on transparent communication, realistic expectations and documented engagement with families and institutions.

Clear boundaries are part of responsible advisory work.

In further detail

Advisory, not transactional. Our work is structured around helping families make better decisions, not around executing transactions on their behalf. Where transactional or regulated execution is required, it is handled by appropriately qualified counterparties.

Coordination with qualified professionals. European jurisdictions reserve certain professional services — legal representation, regulated investment advice, tax filing, immigration legal work — for licensed practitioners. SBMG does not provide these services directly. Where they are required, we coordinate with qualified counterparts in Luxembourg and other European jurisdictions.

No guarantee of outcomes. Admission, permit and employment decisions are made by the relevant institutions and authorities, not by SBMG. We do not present such outcomes as within our control, and we do not accept engagements predicated on guaranteed results.

Documentation and transparency. Our engagements are documented in writing. Fees are agreed in advance. Scope of work is clearly defined. Where the scope changes during an engagement, this is documented before the change takes effect.

Confidentiality. Family information is held in confidence and shared only with parties whose involvement is necessary for the engagement, and only with the family's understanding of such sharing.
