— For schools, universities and institutional partners

Working with schools and universities as long-term partners, not as a recruitment intermediary.

SBMG's work with institutions is grounded in transparency, fit and long-term posture. This page sets out how we approach institutional cooperation, what schools and universities can expect from working with us, and the principles that frame that work.

— Section 01

How we work with institutions

Our relationship with schools and universities is built on a simple premise: we work alongside institutions, not in front of them.

This means we represent the family-side of the conversation. We help families understand a programme's positioning, prepare realistic questions, organise documentation, and engage with admissions teams in a way that respects each institution's process. We do not present ourselves to families as the decision-maker, nor do we present ourselves to institutions as a recruiter.

The work is structured for length, not volume. We expect that the institutions we work with will see a small number of well-prepared families from us, over time, rather than a large pipeline of applications.

— Section 02

Understanding the Chinese-speaking family market

The Chinese-speaking family market is often treated as a single category. In our experience, it is at least three.

Families based in Mainland China make different decisions, on different timelines, with different reference points than families based in Hong Kong, Singapore, North America, Australia or continental Europe. The information environment each family operates in shapes their assumptions about what European education means, what residency offers, and what reasonable timelines look like. These differences matter at the level of admissions — for programme positioning, documentation expectations and communication style.

Our work begins by understanding which environment each family is operating from, and translating between that environment and the institution's actual expectations.

— Section 03

Student counselling approach

Our counselling approach is grounded in fit, not volume. We do not present unsuitable students as strong candidates. Where a family's expectations diverge from what an institution actually offers, we say so directly to the family, not after the application has been submitted.

This posture serves both sides. Families are spared from pursuing pathways that will disappoint them. Institutions are spared from evaluating applications that should not have been submitted.

We do not guarantee admission outcomes. We do not guarantee programme acceptance. We work to ensure that the families who do apply, do so on the basis of accurate understanding and realistic preparation.

— Section 04

Communication and documentation standards

Our communication with institutions is held to a few clear standards: accuracy, verifiability and absence of exaggeration. Family backgrounds, academic records and circumstantial details are represented as they are. We do not improve or rephrase facts that admissions teams would be entitled to verify.

Documentation is prepared to the standard the institution requires, in the language the institution uses, and submitted within the timelines the institution specifies. Where translation is required, it is provided through qualified channels.

— Section 05

Markets we serve

SBMG works with Chinese-speaking families based in Mainland China, Hong Kong, Singapore, Canada, the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand and continental Europe.

Our work covers families considering European education and establishment pathways across multiple stages — from early international school placement through to career-oriented and academic bachelor programmes, and the family-level establishment that often accompanies these decisions.

— Section 06

Institutional dialogue

SBMG is currently building institutional dialogue with selected schools, universities and career-oriented education providers in Switzerland, Luxembourg and other European markets.

We do not present institutions as partners unless a formal relationship has been agreed.

Where institutions are interested in discussing a longer-term working relationship, we welcome that conversation. We expect such conversations to be exploratory at first, structured by shared standards, and grounded in mutual understanding of how the relationship would operate in practice over time.
